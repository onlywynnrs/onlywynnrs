How the PrizePicks & Underdog calculator works

Pick'em DFS platforms like PrizePicks and Underdog Fantasy hide their house edge inside the payout multipliers. A 2-pick Power Play pays 3x — which sounds generous until you realize you need to win two coin-flips in a row (~25% if each leg is a true 50%). This calculator converts those multipliers into the win rate you actually need to break even, then compares it to your estimated hit rate to show whether the entry is +EV or -EV.

Power Play (PrizePicks) and Standard (Underdog) are all-or-nothing: every leg must hit. Flex (PrizePicks) and Insured (Underdog) pay smaller amounts when you miss a leg, lowering your ceiling but cushioning variance. The calculator models both so you can see the real trade-off.

What is expected value (EV) in DFS pick'em?

Expected value is your average profit or loss per entry if you made the same bet thousands of times. Positive EV means the bet makes money long-term; negative EV means it bleeds money no matter how lucky you get short-term. The math is simple: multiply each outcome's payout by its probability, sum them, and subtract your stake. If the result is above zero, the entry is worth making.

How the arbitrage calculator works

Arbitrage betting means backing every outcome of an event across different sportsbooks when their odds disagree enough to guarantee a profit regardless of the result. Enter two opposing prices and the calculator tells you if the combined implied probability is under 100% (the condition for a risk-free arb) and exactly how to split your stake to lock equal profit on both sides. Note: sportsbooks limit or ban accounts that arb consistently, so treat it as a tool, not a long-term plan.

Why most pick'em entries lose

The honest answer: the multipliers are set so that hitting 50/50 legs is a long-term loser. To beat PrizePicks or Underdog you need legs that genuinely clear ~54%+ — which requires comparing their projections to sharp sportsbook lines and finding the mispriced ones.
