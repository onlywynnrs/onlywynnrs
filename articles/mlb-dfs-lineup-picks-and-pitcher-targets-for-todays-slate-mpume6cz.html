Cash Game Stack Recommendations

Stacking remains the optimal approach in MLB DFS, especially for GPP tournaments. The key is identifying lineups facing pitchers with exploitable weaknesses while playing in hitter-friendly conditions.

For cash games, target the top four spots in above-average offenses facing weak pitching. The Minnesota Twins lineup offers solid run-producing potential when they're facing righties with command issues. Their balanced attack from both sides of the plate makes them less vulnerable to bullpen matchups late in games.

When building cash game stacks, prioritize consistency over ceiling. Batters with high contact rates and strong on-base percentages provide safer floors than boom-or-bust power hitters. Look for hitters who've performed well in their recent sample against similar pitch profiles to what they'll face today.

Mini-stacks of 2-3 batters from the same lineup allow you to diversify across multiple games while maintaining correlation upside. Pairing the 1-2 hitters with the cleanup spot gives you exposure to multiple run-scoring scenarios without overcommitting to a single game script.

Tournament GPP Pivot Plays and Contrarian Angles

Winning GPP tournaments requires differentiation from the field. While chalk plays exist for good reason, finding lower-owned options with similar upside separates winning lineups from min-cashes.

Value plays become critical in tournaments when they allow you to afford premium pitching or stack correlations. Batters priced under $3,500 who've recently moved up in the batting order represent excellent leverage opportunities. Lineup spot matters more than salary in many cases, as a $3,000 leadoff hitter provides more fantasy upside than a $4,200 eight-hole batter.

Consider weather-dependent pivots that the masses might overlook. Games with afternoon start times often see lower ownership despite offering the same or better conditions than evening slates. Temperature affects ball flight significantly, with every 10-degree increase adding roughly 1-2 feet of fly ball distance.

Contrarian stacking doesn't mean fading good matchups entirely. Instead, mix one chalky stack with one moderately owned stack and sprinkle in value from low-rostered games. This balanced approach maintains upside while creating unique lineup combinations.

Building Optimal DFS Lineups Across Game Types

Your lineup construction should shift dramatically between cash games and tournaments. Cash game lineups prioritize floor, while GPP lineups maximize ceiling even at the expense of consistency.

For cash games, roster 70% of your salary in your safest plays. This typically means paying up for an ace pitcher and a primary stack from the slate's best matchup. Fill remaining spots with high-floor value rather than chasing upside.

In tournaments, embrace volatility. Roster pitchers with strikeout upside over safe ground-ball specialists. Target power hitters in favorable ballparks rather than contact-oriented table-setters. Accept that tournament lineups will bust more frequently but offer exponentially higher ceilings when they connect.

Always maintain lineup flexibility until lock. Late-breaking news about scratches, weather changes, or lineup adjustments can dramatically shift optimal plays. Building multiple lineup variations allows you to adapt quickly without starting from scratch.
