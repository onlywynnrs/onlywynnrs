Patriots-Seahawks Showdown: Two Kickers at Zero Percent and the Captain Nobody Wants

Super Bowl LX rematch, Lumen Field, 8:20 ET on NBC. Seattle won that game 29-13 and opens as a 3.5-point home favorite.

One game, six roster spots, and roughly 14,000 people building lineups from the same twenty-seven players. That is a fundamentally different problem than a Sunday main slate, and the thing that solves it is ownership, not projections.

How Showdown actually works

If you have only played classic slates, the format changes three things.

You roster six players from a single game, one of whom is your Captain. The Captain scores 1.5 times his points and costs 1.5 times his salary. You must include at least one player from each team, and the cap is the usual $50,000.

That 1.5x multiplier is the entire game. Your Captain choice matters more than your other five picks combined, because it is the only slot where you get a different return than everyone else holding the same player. Two lineups can share five names and finish thousands of places apart on the Captain alone.

What the field is doing

Projected rostership on this slate is extraordinarily concentrated.

Sam Darnold sits at 81.3 percent. Drake Maye at 72.6. Jaxon Smith-Njigba at 65.4, A.J. Brown at 58.9, Rhamondre Stevenson at 56.3. Five players will appear in more than half of all lineups, and both starting quarterbacks are near-universal.

Below that it collapses fast. Hunter Henry is 7.4 percent. Rashid Shaheed 6.7. Cooper Kupp 5.1. Corey Kiner is one-tenth of one percent. And both kickers are projected at exactly zero.

That shape is normal for a single-game slate and it is where the entire edge is.

The two kickers nobody is rostering

Jason Myers costs $5,400 and projects for 8.85 points. That is more than Cooper Kupp, more than either defense, more than DeMario Douglas. Andy Borregales costs $5,000 and projects 7.61.

Both are at zero percent rostership.

Kickers are the most reliably underowned scoring source in Showdown because they feel unserious. But the format does not care how a point was scored. A kicker in a competitive game with two good offenses is a stable mid-single-digit floor with a real path to double digits, and in this one you can have that production while sharing it with nobody.

This is not a clever contrarian dart. It is a player projected for more than several rostered options, available at zero ownership, in a format where duplication is the thing that kills you.

Hunter Henry and A.J. Barner are the same play at half the price in ownership

Look at these two side by side.

Hunter Henry: $4,800, projected 9.42, rostered 7.4 percent.

A.J. Barner: $4,600, projected 9.34, rostered 14.5 percent.

Eight hundredths of a point apart in projection. Two hundred dollars apart in salary. And Barner will appear in twice as many lineups.

Barner also carries an oblique designation on the injury report. He practiced fully and has no game status, so this is not a reason to avoid him outright — but when two players are functionally identical and one has a note next to his name and double the ownership, the choice makes itself.

Henderson being out cuts both ways

TreVeyon Henderson is officially ruled out with an ankle injury. Rhamondre Stevenson starts, with Corey Kiner as the backup.

The field has processed the first half of that. Stevenson at 56.3 percent rostership is chalk precisely because Henderson is out, and the projection of 16.51 supports it.

The half nobody has processed is Kiner. He is $2,400 and rostered by one lineup in a thousand. If New England splits the backfield at all, or if Kiner takes short-yardage work, that is a two-thousand-dollar roster spot returning real points at effectively zero ownership. It is a dart, and it should be treated as one. But on a slate this concentrated, one dart is how you separate.

The broader read matters too. Losing Henderson takes explosiveness out of the New England run game, which pushes them toward the air in a hostile building. That is directionally good for Maye, Brown, and the pass-catchers, and it is already reflected in their ownership.

The trap at the bottom of the salary sheet

Elijah Arroyo is $1,600, projects 3.91, and is rostered at 26.9 percent. Eli Raridon is $1,800, projects 4.57, and sits at 18.8 percent.

On a value basis they lead the slate — 2.44 and 2.54 points per thousand dollars. That number is doing something misleading, though. Both are cheap enough that almost any production produces a gaudy ratio, and neither is projected to clear five points.

More than a quarter of the field is rostering a player projected for under four points. That is not a value play, it is everyone reaching for salary relief in the same spot at the same time. If you need the savings, take Raridon over Arroyo — better projection, better price-adjusted output, and eight points less ownership.

Where the Captain leverage lives

With both quarterbacks above 70 percent rostership, captaining Darnold or Maye puts you in an enormous crowd. It might still be correct. It will not be unique.

Two builds worth considering, both legal and both under the cap:

A.J. Brown captain — Brown at CPT, with Maye, Stevenson, Henry, Myers, and Shaheed. $49,400 spent, projects 89.4, cumulative rostership 202 percent.

Rhamondre Stevenson captain — Stevenson at CPT, with Maye, Brown, Henry, Myers, and Kupp. $47,600 spent, projects 87.7, cumulative rostership 200 percent.

Compare either to the obvious build: Darnold captain with Smith-Njigba, Maye, Brown, Arroyo, and Raridon. That projects 90.9 — higher — at 324 percent cumulative rostership. You gain roughly two projected points and take on 120 percentage points of additional duplication risk.

In a large single-game field, that is a bad trade. The chalk build wins the projection contest and shares first place with everyone else who ran the optimizer.

The short version

Captain away from the quarterbacks. Take Henry over Barner. Put a kicker in the lineup and stop feeling strange about it. Use Raridon if you need the punt, not Arroyo. And if you want one lottery ticket, Kiner at $2,400 is the cheapest real one on the board.

Ownership figures here are projected rostership, not measured results, and projections move when inactives post. Check before lock.
