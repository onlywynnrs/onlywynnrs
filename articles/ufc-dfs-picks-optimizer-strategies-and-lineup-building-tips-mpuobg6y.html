Cash Game vs GPP Strategy for UFC Slates

Cash game lineups demand consistency. Target fighters with high floor projections—grapplers who rack up control time, volume strikers in favorable matchups, and favorites with decision upside. Your optimizer should weight projection floors heavily for double-ups and head-to-heads.

The Islam Makhachev vs Ilia Topuria bout exemplifies this decision tree. Makhachev's dominant grappling typically produces safe fantasy scores through takedowns and control, making him a cash consideration despite likely high ownership. Topuria brings explosive finishing ability but carries more risk. In cash games, you're paying for certainty. In GPPs, you're buying differentiation.

For tournaments, recalibrate your optimizer to emphasize ceiling outcomes and lower-owned fighters. The Ketlen Souza vs Ariane Carnelossi matchup in the women's strawweight division might fly under the radar, but both fighters have finishing ability at reasonable salaries. These are the plays that win GPPs when chalk fails—not necessarily the best fighters, but the best combination of upside and ownership leverage.

Salary Cap Optimization and Stacking Theory

Effective UFC DFS requires ruthless salary allocation. Every dollar wasted on marginal upgrades is a dollar you can't spend on fight-winning upside elsewhere. Run multiple optimizer iterations with different constraint sets to explore the salary spectrum.

Consider punt plays strategically. If you're rostering expensive favorites, you'll need value elsewhere. Look for live underdogs in competitive matchups where the betting line might be tighter than public perception suggests. The goal isn't finding sure winners at minimum salary—it's finding fighters whose range of outcomes justifies their price tag.

Stacking in UFC differs from other sports. You're not correlating teammates, but rather betting on fight dynamics. If you believe a main card fight goes to decision, both fighters score. If you expect a quick finish, the winner explodes while the loser busts. Your optimizer should reflect these scenarios through correlation matrices, not just independent projections.
