Optimal Lineup Construction Strategies

Most DFS optimizers default to cash game or tournament modes, but understanding the difference is crucial. Cash game lineups prioritize floor and consistency—you want players with secure roles and predictable volume. Tournament lineups require ceiling projection and lower ownership to differentiate from the field.

For tournaments, consider game stacking strategies. Pairing a quarterback with his pass catchers, then adding a running back or receiver from the opposing team capitalizes on potential shootouts. The Chicago Bears vs Minnesota Vikings matchup could present stacking opportunities depending on the game environment and how Vegas projects scoring.

Salary allocation is another critical component. A balanced approach typically works for cash games, but tournaments often benefit from stars-and-scrubs builds—paying up for elite players while finding minimum-salary value to fill out your roster. This week's slate includes several salary-saving options at running back and tight end that smart players will exploit.

Advanced Optimizer Customization Techniques

The difference between average and elite DFS players often comes down to optimizer customization. Don't accept default projections—adjust based on your own research and contrarian angles.

Start by adjusting ownership projections. If everyone expects a certain quarterback to be chalky, your optimizer should account for that when building tournament lineups. Lower projected ownership on comparable plays creates leverage opportunities.

Next, customize player exposure limits. In tournaments, you want diversification across multiple lineups while maintaining exposure to your highest-conviction plays. Set minimum exposure floors on your value plays that you believe the field will under-utilize.

The Houston Texans vs Cincinnati Bengals game might feature players with inflated ownership due to name recognition. By slightly reducing exposure to obvious plays and increasing it on overlooked value options, you create differentiation without sacrificing expected points.

Finally, use correlation rules effectively. Stack players who benefit from the same game script while avoiding negative correlations like opposing running backs. The most sophisticated DFS players understand how game environments create interconnected player outcomes.
