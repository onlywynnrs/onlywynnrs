UFC Vegas 120 DFS Deep Dive: Gamrot vs Salkilld Strategy, Picks & Leverage

UFC Vegas 120 Deep Dive: Gamrot's Wrestling Math, the Mid-Priced Main Event, and Where This Slate Gets Won

The UFC comes home to the Apex this Saturday after three straight international trips — 12 fights, prelims at 5 p.m. ET, main card at 8 on Paramount+, with UFC 330 looming one week later. The main event is the card's best puzzle: eighth-ranked Mateusz Gamrot, one of the division's elite wrestlers, is a +125 underdog to Quillan Salkilld, a 26-year-old Australian riding a twelve-fight win streak who just knocked out Beneil Dariush. One housekeeping note first: Jesus Rosas shows on the DraftKings sheet with no fight — he's off the card. Don't roster him. Now the full read.

The Main Event: A Style Clash the Scoring System Cares About

Start with what the numbers say. Salkilld lands 5.01 significant strikes per minute to Gamrot's 3.21 — he's the volume and the power. Gamrot's game is the other axis entirely: takedowns at a 38% clip with 83% takedown defense, and a five-round gas tank that's been proven at the top of the division.

Here's the detail the market noticed: in the Dariush win — the one that made Salkilld's name — he was dropped by a jab and taken down twice by a faded veteran. Composure got him through it, but showing a wrestling leak right before fighting Mateusz Gamrot is like showing a cracked window before a storm. The line has moved accordingly: Salkilld opened around -142 and has tightened to roughly -125/+125 — money quietly arriving on the wrestler.

The DFS translation is where it gets interesting. Salkilld's 112.4 FPPF is real but must be sized honestly — it's built on a three-fight UFC sample, our standing rule applies, and his ceiling comes from knockouts. Gamrot's 86.0 is the opposite: a large-sample, floor-driven number, because takedowns score five points each and Gamrot piles them up. And unlike most wrestling dogs, a Gamrot win isn't a quiet score — five rounds of takedown accumulation plus a decision is a genuinely strong DFS line. At $7,900 versus $8,300, you're paying $400 less for the side the money is moving toward, whose winning method the scoring system pays directly. Salkilld remains the finish-equity side; Gamrot is the best combination of price, direction, and verified output in the fight. They're opponents — one per lineup, and this is a fight worth taking a side on in every build.

The Structural Quirk: A Main Event Priced Like a Mid-Card Fight

Both main-eventers cost less than $8,400. That's rare — and it reshapes the slate. Normally the marquee fight eats $9,000+ of your cap; this week the premium tier ($9,000-$9,600) is entirely prelim-and-midcard favorites: del Valle, Foro, Ty Miller, Sousa, Asplund. The field will spend up there because the cap allows it. Which makes the premium tier worth auditing honestly:

Ty Miller ($9,200, -339) — a 124.9 FPPF that leads the slate, with the caveat that it's a short UFC sample; 7-0 with fast finishes is a real profile, priced accordingly.

Manoel Sousa ($9,100, -264) — here's a quiet gap: DK priced him like a -400 favorite, but the market says -264. A 98.5 FPPF makes him rosterable; just know you're paying a premium the odds don't fully back.

Carol Foro ($9,300, -212) — the Tuchalov warning, again: near-top salary for a debutant with zero UFC data, and her market number (-212) is the softest in the premium tier. Seven straight wins is real pedigree; $9,300 for no sample while the market prices a 68% favorite is a pass by our rules.

del Valle ($9,600, -400) — the most expensive and most market-backed. Justified chalk if you pay up.

The Elkins Problem: When Verified Output Is a Trap

We preach verified-output dogs every week, so here's the discipline check: Darren Elkins, $6,600, carries a 73.8 FPPF — nearly matching del Valle's 75.9 at $3,000 less. Free money? No. The market prices him +584 — about a 15% dog. That career average was built across a decade of bloody wars, and "The Damage" is now 40+ fighting a surging 10-1 prospect. The FPPF column doesn't age-adjust; the betting market does. This is the week's reminder that our verified-output rule has a second clause: the output must be current. Elkins ownership will be sentimental, not sharp. Let the field carry it.

The Real Leverage: Quarantillo at +161

The co-main is where the rule applies correctly. Billy Quarantillo ($7,500) averages 79.7 FPPF — higher than the favorite Ferreira's 65.2 — at $1,200 less, as a +161 dog. His style is the reason: relentless pace, volume, scrambles, exactly the profile that scores whether or not he wins rounds cleanly. Ferreira at 41 is the aging side of an unranked lightweight fight. This is the closest thing to the Rodriguez play from Belgrade: a dog whose scoring history is better than his price, in a fight the field will skip past. Best leverage on the card.

Honorable mentions in the dog tier: Amanda Lemos (+226) is the name-recognition dog against the surging Thainara — Thainara's 109.9 FPPF carries a short-sample caveat but her trajectory is real; Lemos is live, not preferred. Gianni Vazquez (+155) faces a Miles Johns whose line softened from our early read (-155 now) — worth watching into lock. And Jose Montanha (+178) is the debutant dart with a live number against Sutherland's modest 41.2 output.

The Debutant File

Four unknowns after the Rosas scratch: Foro ($9,300), Canuto ($6,900), Miranda ($7,100), Montanha ($7,700) — and Foro vs. Canuto is this card's double-debut fight, the 2 p.m. PT opener, with Canuto's record showing heavy first-round finish rates in her regional run. Doctrine unchanged: one unknown per lineup, take the one whose line moves toward him late. As of now that's Montanha at +178 — check the board Saturday.

The Build Doctrine for Vegas 120

Pick a side of the main event in every lineup — it's the rare marquee fight where both sides are mid-priced and playable, and the wrestler's method actually scores. Audit the premium tier with sample discipline (Miller and del Valle over Foro; know what you're paying for with Sousa). Take Quarantillo as the leverage centerpiece. Respect the Elkins lesson: verified output must be current output. And spend the salary the cheap main event hands you on real numbers, not debutant assumptions.
