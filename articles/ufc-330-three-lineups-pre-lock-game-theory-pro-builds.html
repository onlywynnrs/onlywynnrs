UFC 330: Three Lineups to Take Down the $600K — A Pro's Pre-Lock Card

Three Lineups for the $600K: A Pro's Final Card, Hours From Lock

Let's start with the only fully honest sentence in DFS: nobody knows what happens tonight. Not us, not the sharps, not the books. Twelve fights is twelve coin-ish flips weighted by skill, and variance eats certainty for breakfast. What a professional actually does isn't predict — it's price. You figure out where 28,000 entries are paying too much attention, where they're paying too little, and you position so that when the chaos lands — and it always lands — it lands on your numbers. Here's the final board, the last reassessment, and the three lineups we'd fire.

The Final Board Delivered One Last Gift

The biggest pre-lock move on the card: Kaue Fernandes is no longer an underdog. He opened the week +155 against Jalin Turner; he closes at -104, the favorite — while still priced at $7,600, a full $1,000 below Turner. We've tracked salary-versus-market gaps for nine weeks and this is the cleanest completed flip yet: the market spent all week agreeing with the output columns (86.3 FPPF vs. a fading 76.8) until the price inverted entirely. The cost of confirmation is ownership — he won't be a secret tonight — but a favorite at underdog salary is a structural edge that survives being noticed.

The rest of the close: the Johnson-Chapolin banner fight settled at a dead coinflip (-120/+102, both under $8K — the cheapest live-equity fight on the board). Robertson kept taking title-fight money (+164 while Dern drifted to -198). Luque-Gore locked in as a pure pick'em. Makhachev firmed to -360. And the premium tier held its steepness: Orolbai -1100, Ribovics -700, Abdul-Malik -650.

Overvalued vs. Undervalued: The Final Map

What the field is overvaluing tonight: Charles Johnson (the banner manufactured his ownership; the market says his fight is a coinflip, not a value play). Edson Barboza at +505 (nineteen years of highlights buying 17% equity). Mackenzie Dern (champion + name + face ownership on a line that softened all week). And Makhachev's score — his win is likely; a wide-decision win that underwhelms against Garry's movement is nearly as likely as the finish everyone's rostering him for.

What the field is undervaluing: Chapolin (identical equity to Johnson, a fraction of the attention). Robertson (the only title-fight dog whose losing floor still pays — scrambles score). Brahimaj (-149 in the fight the field literally forgets exists; the Magny name takes what little ownership the fight draws). Alvarez's hidden kicker (his opponent needed two trips to the scale Friday — brutal cuts don't improve overnight). And unspent-salary flexibility itself: with real equity living under $8K tonight, the mid-tier is structurally under-owned as the field barbells.

The Three Lineups

Built as one player, shared spine, the main-event fork covered two-to-one, the banner coinflip hedged across entries. Spine in all three: Orolbai (the safest points on the board at -1100) and Fernandes (the completed flip).

Lineup 1 — The Takedown ($49,700): Makhachev · Orolbai · Fernandes · Chapolin · Robertson · Brahimaj. Chalk where the field is right, alone where equity is even. Three surgical deviations — Chapolin over Johnson, Robertson under the Dern pile, Brahimaj in the forgotten fight — separate this entry from roughly 80% of the field while surrendering almost no win probability. On a normal-chaos night, this is the first-place shape.

Lineup 2 — The Mirror ($50,000): Makhachev · Orolbai · Fernandes · Charles Johnson · Luque · Alvarez. Same spine, opposite side of the banner fight — between L1 and L2 we own the Johnson-Chapolin winner with certainty, which is the whole point of a hedge. Luque at his earned pick'em price (the week the output-inversion rule beat the aging-name rule), and Alvarez carrying the weight-cut kicker.

Lineup 3 — The Apocalypse ($49,700): Garry · Orolbai · Fernandes · Abdul-Malik · Robertson · Donte Johnson. The branch where 70% of the field dies simultaneously. Nobody builds this lineup because they think Garry wins — you build it because if he does, the tournament resets with your entry near the top of what's left, fattened by premium chalk (-650, -305) that doesn't care what happened in the main event. One in three entries on the 28% outcome is a portfolio decision, not a prediction.

Portfolio totals: zero Barboza, zero Magny, zero Wells at +709, zero $9,100 debutant — every dollar of the field's name tax and banner ownership works for these three. Robertson twice, because chaos nights are rarely chaotic in only one fight.
