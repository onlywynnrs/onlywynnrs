Fantasy Football Snake Draft Strategy by Pick Position 2024

Understanding Snake Draft Positioning Advantages

Your draft slot determines your entire fantasy football strategy before you even make your first pick. In a standard 12-team snake draft, picking first gives you the best player available but leaves you waiting 23 picks before your next selection. Meanwhile, the turn positions (picks 6-7) offer more balanced roster construction with shorter waits between picks. The key is maximizing the inherent advantages of your specific slot while minimizing the drawbacks.

Early picks (1-4) should prioritize the elite tier at running back or wide receiver, targeting the true difference-makers who provide a significant advantage over replacement-level players. Your second and third round picks become crucial for filling positional gaps and securing reliable starters before the mid-tier talent depletes. Late first-round picks (9-12) benefit from back-to-back selections, allowing you to secure two solid starters or lock up a position entirely by taking two running backs or wide receivers consecutively.

Early Draft Slot Strategy (Picks 1-4)

Securing a top-four pick means landing an elite running back or wide receiver who consistently delivers weekly ceiling performances. The challenge is navigating the long wait until your next pick in the late second round. During this gap, approximately 20-24 players disappear from the board, often depleting an entire position's top tier.

Your second and third round picks should address roster balance. If you opened with a running back, consider the best available wide receiver on the turn, or vice versa. Avoid reaching for positional needs too early—let the draft come to you in rounds two and three, then become more aggressive about filling gaps in the middle rounds. By round five, you should have at least two running backs and two wide receivers, giving you flexibility to target value at quarterback or tight end.

Middle Draft Slot Strategy (Picks 5-8)

The middle picks offer the most balanced draft experience with manageable waits between selections. You'll miss the truly elite tier-one players, but you can construct a well-rounded roster without glaring weaknesses. Focus on accumulating solid RB2 and WR2 types who provide safe floors with upside potential.

This draft slot rewards aggressive zero-RB or robust-RB strategies depending on how the board falls. If running backs fly off early, don't reach just to fill a position. Wide receiver depth typically extends deeper into drafts, so loading up on receiver value early can pay dividends. Alternatively, if others zig toward receivers, secure two quality running backs with your first two picks and dominate the scarcity at that position.

Late Draft Slot Strategy (Picks 9-12)

The turn picks provide a unique advantage: back-to-back selections that let you control roster construction. Many championship teams come from the back of drafts because of this flexibility. The key is identifying which positions are about to experience a run and positioning yourself accordingly.

With picks at the end of rounds, you can see trends developing and react. If tight ends start disappearing in round four, you can grab one on your 4-5 turn. If everyone loads up on wide receivers early, you can corner the running back market with your first four picks. This reactive strategy requires staying flexible and avoiding rigid predraft plans.

Consider your league's specific settings when drafting from the turn. In PPR formats, wide receiver value increases, making a receiver-heavy start viable. In standard scoring, running back scarcity makes securing two early backs crucial. By round six, you should have a clear starter at every position and be targeting high-upside bench stashes and handcuffs.

Adjusting Your Strategy Throughout the Draft

Regardless of starting position, successful snake drafts require constant adjustment. Track position runs—when three or four consecutive players at one position go off the board—because these create value elsewhere. If everyone panics and reaches for quarterbacks, don't follow unless you're getting truly elite production.

The middle rounds (6-10) are where leagues are won or lost. This is when you balance floor and ceiling, securing safe starters while adding upside lottery tickets. Target players in new systems, those returning from injury with reduced ADPs, or backups one injury away from weekly starting roles. Late-round tight ends and quarterbacks often provide 80% of the production of early-round picks at those positions, making patience valuable.
