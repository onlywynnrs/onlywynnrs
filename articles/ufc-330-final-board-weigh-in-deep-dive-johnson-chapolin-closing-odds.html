UFC 330, Weighed In: The Final Board, the Glitch That Closed, and Where the Money Finished

Everyone made weight in Philadelphia — all 24 fighters, both title fights official, Michael Morales on standby at 170 as main-event insurance. One scale note with DFS teeth: Chidi Njokuani needed two attempts, missing at 171.5 before making 171 behind the curtain. A hard cut the day before a fight against Joel Alvarez — whose line has firmed to -320 — is a flag, not a footnote.

This is the final deep dive with everything live: what the closing market did to our ownership-physics reads, starting with the one that changed most. When our centerpiece call moves, we lead with it. That's the whole deal here.

The Glitch Closed — and Turned Into Something Better

Our first UFC 330 piece was built around Charles Johnson: priced $6,900 as a +280 dog against Ochoa, flipped to a -245 favorite when Eduardo "Chapolin" Henrique stepped in short-notice. We called it the largest salary-versus-market gap we'd ever tracked.

The market has since erased most of it. Johnson closes around -128, Chapolin at +104 — the books met Chapolin, watched him make the 130-pound catchweight without drama (both men hit 129.5), and priced this a near coinflip. The honest scoreboard on our own call: the structural observation was right, the fight equity behind it shrank by the day, and anyone who built early around "Johnson the 71% favorite" is holding a different ticket than they thought.

Here's what it turned into, though — and it's arguably more interesting: an entire fight priced under $8,000 a side, at essentially even odds. Johnson $6,900, Chapolin $7,900. On a PPV slate where the premium tier runs to $9,600, this is the cheapest live-equity fight on the board — you can own a coinflip winner for punt-tier salary in either direction. Johnson remains the marginally cheaper side of a 50/50; Chapolin is the short-notice unknown the market just spent a week upgrading, which is itself a signal (money doesn't move toward mystery without a reason). Doctrine: this fight is now a pick-a-side spot like a Temirov-Erceg, not a glitch to exploit — and taking the $6,900 side leaves the most cap on the table.

The Steam Report: Two of Our Quiet Plays Got Loud

Kaue Fernandes: +155 → +112. The leverage centerpiece got exactly the confirmation Temirov got in Abu Dhabi — the market bet our output-inversion read (86.3 FPPF vs. Turner's fading 76.8) nearly to even money. He's no longer a secret, but at $7,600 he's still priced like one.

Gillian Robertson: +190 → +170. Late money on the title-fight dog while Dern softened from -240 to -205. The submission-versus-submission coinflip is closer than the week opened, and Robertson remains the rare dog whose losing scores still count.

And the Luque tension resolved itself. We wrote that he triggered both our inversion rule (81.9 FPPF, higher than Gore's 51.4) and our Elkins rule (historical output, accumulated damage). The market picked a side: Luque closed from +140 to a dead pick'em (-105/-115). When the money erases the dog price on the higher-output side, the inversion rule wins the argument. Upgraded from "small exposure" to a legitimate play — with the caveat that his ownership will rise with the line move.

The Premium Tier at Close: Steeper Than We Wrote It

The real lines came in heavier than the early board: Orolbai closes at -1100 — the biggest favorite on the card, not merely its most expensive fighter. Ribovics -700 (Barboza out to +505 — the name tax got more expensive to pay). Abdul-Malik -650. Donte Johnson -295. The premium chalk is more market-backed than our midweek numbers suggested, which cuts two ways: safer floors up top, and even less reason for the field to differentiate there — reinforcing that this tournament is won in the sub-$8K tier.

Two honest downgrades that follow: Jeremiah Wells at +709 is no longer "the dart with a track record" — at 12% implied, his 79.3 FPPF is an Elkins-zone number now, a true moonshot only. And Lucas Fernando softened again (-280), keeping the premium-debutant archetype's streak alive: four cards running, the market has cooled on the expensive unknown by lock. The $9,100 pass stands.

The Main Event at Close

Makhachev firmed slightly to -360; Garry drifted to +285. No drama, no weight issues, and the backup weighed in clean. The gravity-well read holds exactly as written: the champ is table stakes at what will be enormous ownership, his wrestling-and-submission profile is what the scoring pays, and the Garry pivot ($7,000, real 82.6 FPPF, now at a slightly better price than midweek) still belongs in exactly one build of any portfolio. Njokuani's scale struggle is the last note: the market firmed Alvarez to -320 before the weigh-in wobble, and nothing about a brutal cut argues the other way.

The Final Assembly

For Saturday's $600K field, weighed in and live:

The premium tier is safer and duller than ever — anchor with the market-backed trio (Orolbai at his -1100, Abdul-Malik, Ribovics) sized to your risk, and keep paying the Makhachev toll in most builds. The middle is where the week's honest updates concentrate: Fernandes at his steam-confirmed +112 is the priority play, Luque graduates to a real roster spot at his pick'em price, Robertson carries the title-fight leverage. The bottom is the Johnson-Chapolin coinflip — pick a side, bank the savings, and remember the $6,900 side buys the same 50/50 for $1,000 less. Let Barboza's +505 and the Magny coinflip keep collecting the name tax from 28,000 casual entries. One debutant maximum, and after four straight weeks of the pattern, the expensive one isn't the one.
