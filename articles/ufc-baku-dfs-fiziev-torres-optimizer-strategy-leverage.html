UFC Baku DFS Strategy: Fiziev vs Torres Optimizer Picks & Leverage

UFC Baku Is a Finish-Hunter's Slate

DraftKings MMA scoring rewards one thing above all else: finishes. A first-round finish is worth +90 points, plus a +25 quick-win bonus if it comes inside 60 seconds. A decision win? Just +30. That gap is the entire game. On a card like UFC Fight Night: Fiziev vs. Torres in Baku, where a huge share of the fighters are aggressive finishers, your job in the $50,000 salary cap is simple to say and hard to do: roster six fighters who can end the night early.

This is a Dagestan- and Azerbaijan-heavy card built around local stars, which means a lot of high-finish-rate favorites and a few live underdogs with real knockout power. Here is how we are reading the player pool.

The Ceiling Plays Worth Paying Up For

A few fighters carry the kind of finishing résumé that justifies a premium price.

Manuel Torres ($8,000) is the single most interesting roster spot on the slate. Sixteen of his seventeen career wins have come in the first round, and all of his UFC fights have ended in the opening frame. He is priced as a near pick'em against Rafael Fiziev in the main event, yet his salary sits in the middle of the pack. That is a mispriced ceiling. The risk: it is a five-round main event, and if Fiziev survives the early storm and drags it long, Torres' fantasy floor craters. He is a tournament play, not a safe one.

Javier Reyes ($8,500) carries the highest scoring average on the board and projects as a finisher in a favorable spot. Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev ($9,500) and Daniil Donchenko ($9,400) are heavily favored local prospects with big finishing upside — premium anchors if you want chalk that can actually post a tournament-winning score rather than grind a decision.

Shara Magomedov ($9,200), the co-main attraction, is a -340 type favorite with highlight-reel finishing ability. He is the cleanest "anchor" on the slate, and pairing him with a leverage dog is a classic build.

Where the Leverage Lives

Tournaments are won by being different where it counts. The finish-heavy scoring means a live underdog who lands one clean shot can outscore a favorite who wins a boring decision — at a fraction of the ownership.

Michel Pereira ($7,000) is the headline leverage play. He is an underdog against Shara Magomedov, but his acrobatic, knockout-and-knee-strike style means he is never out of a fight. At this salary, he is exactly the kind of cheap finisher who lets you afford two premium anchors.

Michal Oleksiejczuk ($7,900) is close to a coin-flip on the live dog side with real power, and Kaan Ofli ($7,700) offers a contrarian angle if you think the price on Reyes is too short.

A Word on the Debutants

Several fighters on this card — including Farman Hasanov, Tahir Abdullayev, and the prospects without DraftKings scoring history — have no track record in this scoring system. That makes them genuine unknowns. In a large-field tournament, an unknown finisher can be a leverage dart. In a cash game, the lack of data is a reason to fade. Know which game you are playing before you click them in.

Building the Lineup

The honest framework for a $100K-to-first tournament on a finish-heavy card: anchor with one or two premium finishers (a Yakhyaev, Donchenko, or Shara Magomedov), find your value at the bottom with live dogs who can finish (Pereira is the model here), and make sure every roster spot has a realistic path to a finish. Decision-only fighters are salary you cannot afford to waste.

For cash games, lean harder into the heavy favorites with finishing pedigree and accept a lower ceiling for a safer floor. For the big tournament, take the Torres-type swings — the mispriced finisher whose ceiling can win you the whole thing.

One thing we will never do: pretend we know exactly how these fights play out. We do not sell certainty. We read the market, weigh the finishing data, and show our work. Fights are chaos. The edge is in stacking probability, not predicting the future.
