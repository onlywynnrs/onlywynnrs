How to Beat Polymarket: Finding Mispriced Sports Contracts With True Probability

Prediction markets have quietly become one of the biggest stories in sports. Combined monthly volume across the major platforms climbed from under five billion dollars in late 2025 to roughly twenty-four billion by spring 2026, and sports now make up the majority of open interest. If you bet sports and you are not at least watching what is happening on prediction markets, you are ignoring where a large and growing share of the smart money is going.

But here is what most people miss about platforms like Polymarket: the prices are not odds in the traditional sense. They are probabilities. A contract trading at forty cents is the market saying there is a forty percent chance the outcome happens. That single fact changes how you find an edge — and it happens to be exactly what our engine is built to measure.

Why prediction-market pricing creates opportunity

A traditional sportsbook bakes a margin into its odds — the vig — so the implied probabilities across both sides add up to more than one hundred percent. Prediction-market contracts are cleaner: a yes and a no should sum to roughly a dollar. That makes it easier to compare the market's stated probability against your own honest estimate of the true probability. When those two numbers diverge, you have found a potential edge.

The whole game becomes: what is the real probability of this outcome, and where is the market's price wrong about it?

Where the market is predictably wrong

Prediction markets are made up of people, and people have biases. The most reliable one is the brand premium. Popular, high-profile teams get overpriced because casual money piles onto names it recognizes, regardless of the actual matchup. Small-market teams and underdogs get systematically underpriced for the same reason in reverse. A contract is not priced on win probability alone — it is priced on win probability plus how many people want that team to win.

That gap between perception and reality is the inefficiency. A market that prices a glamour franchise at a number their on-court performance does not justify is handing an edge to anyone willing to do the math instead of following the crowd.

How to actually find it

The method is the same one we use for every pick on OnlyWynnrs, just pointed at a prediction-market contract instead of a sportsbook line:

First, strip the noise and estimate the true probability. We do this by de-vigging the sharpest available moneylines across multiple books to back out a clean, no-margin probability for each side. That gives you a baseline the crowd's sentiment has not touched.

Second, compare that true probability to the contract price. If our model says a team is a fifty-five percent favorite and the prediction-market contract is trading at forty-six cents, the market is underpricing them by nine points. That is a buy. If the model says forty percent and the contract is trading at fifty cents on the back of brand-name hype, that is an overpriced contract worth fading.

Third, factor in line movement. When the sharpest sportsbooks move a line in a direction the prediction-market price has not caught up to yet, that lag is a window. Markets do not all update at the same speed, and the slower one is where the value sits.

The Finals are a perfect example

Right now the NBA Finals are live, and a deep playoff series is exactly the kind of event where prediction-market volume spikes and casual sentiment runs hottest — which means mispricing runs hottest too. Series-winner contracts, individual game contracts, and live in-game markets all move on emotion as much as logic during a Finals run. The team with the bigger national fanbase will tend to carry a brand premium in its contract price. The disciplined play is to ignore who you want to win, calculate the true probability from the sharpest lines, and trade only the gaps.
