UFC 330 DFS Deep Dive: The Ownership Physics of the $600K Special

The Ownership Physics of UFC 330: A $600K Field Study

This one's structured differently, because a PPV slate is a different animal. The $600K UFC 330 Special (28,235 entries, $200K to first) will be the most casual-heavy field we've covered — title fights pull in entrants who build one lineup around the names they know from the poster. That changes the physics: ownership stops tracking win probability and starts tracking fame. So instead of walking the card fight by fight, this is a map of where those 28,000 entries will pile up, where the pile is wrong, and where the quiet money lives. Philadelphia, Saturday, main card 9 p.m. ET.

The Gravity Well: Makhachev at $9,200

Every PPV slate has one fighter who bends the whole field around him. Islam Makhachev — pound-for-pound No. 1, sixteen straight wins, first defense of the welterweight belt he took from Jack Della Maddalena — is priced at $9,200, below three prelim favorites. That's not a discount you'll get to enjoy alone: he'll be the highest-owned fighter on the slate by a distance.

The honest math says the ownership is mostly deserved. A 99.0 FPPF built on wrestling control and submission threat is exactly what the scoring pays, at -340. The crack in the well: Ian Machado Garry's entire game — length, footwork, distance management — is designed to make fights low-output, and even Tom Aspinall is calling this Islam's toughest recent test. A five-round decision where Garry circles and survives is a fine Makhachev win and a mediocre Makhachev score. You play him; you just don't get an edge from him. In a 28K field, the champ is table stakes, not a pick. And Garry at $7,000 with a real 82.6 FPPF is the pivot that ruins everyone's night if "The Future" arrives a fight early — one lineup in a portfolio should own that outcome.

The Glitch: Charles Johnson at $6,900

Here's the strangest number on the board, and the user of any salary sheet should understand exactly why it exists. DraftKings priced Johnson at $6,900 when he was a +280 underdog to Jose Ochoa. Then Ochoa withdrew injured, Eduardo Henrique stepped in on short notice at a 130-pound catchweight — and the market installed Johnson as a -245 favorite. The salary never moved. That's a fighter priced as a 26% dog who is now a 71% favorite: the largest salary-versus-market gap we've tracked in eight weeks of doing this.

Two honesty checks before you sprint to click him. First, the reasons he was cheap are real: Johnson is 35, this is his fourth fight in twelve months, he's 2-3 in his last five, and the two recent losses were ugly — dropped twice and finished by Perez, controlled for nearly nine minutes and submitted by Almabayev. Second, this is not a secret: DraftKings runs a banner on the contest page announcing the change. The field will see it, which means the glitch converts from leverage into chalk — a $6,900 fighter with favorite equity and heavy ownership. Play him anyway. When the structural edge is this large, you take it at any ownership; your differentiation has to come from somewhere else. Which brings us to—

The Name Tax: Where the Casuals Overpay

PPV fields click names, and this card is stacked with famous fading ones. Edson Barboza (+380, $6,800) — nineteen years of highlight reels, now a 17% dog to Ribovics in a fight the previews call a firefight. Vicente Luque ($7,800, +140) — the most interesting tension on the board, because he triggers both of our rules at once: the output-inversion rule loves him (81.9 FPPF, higher than favorite Gore's 51.4, at $600 less), and the Elkins rule warns you (that average is historical; the recent tape and accumulated damage are the concern). Verdict: unlike Elkins at +571, Luque at +140 is genuinely live — small exposure is defensible, heavy exposure is nostalgia. Neil Magny ($8,000, coinflip vs. Brahimaj) — the all-time welterweight wins list personified, in a fight sharp entrants will simply skip. The tax these three collect from the field is your rebate: every point of ownership they absorb makes the quiet plays quieter.

The Quiet Money: Three Numbers the Field Won't Read

Kaue Fernandes, $7,600, +155, 86.3 FPPF — higher than the favorite he's fighting. Jalin Turner is 15-9 with a ceiling everyone remembers and a consistency problem everyone forgets. This is the cleanest output inversion on the card and the leverage centerpiece of the week, playing the same role Quarantillo did at Vegas 120.

Gillian Robertson, $7,200, +190, 77.6 FPPF. A title-fight dog whose submission game is real. The honest counterweight: Dern just spent a title fight proving she can eat nine takedowns and win anyway, and the analyst consensus says Robertson has no Plan B standing. But grappler-versus-grappler fights produce scrambles, and scrambles produce points — this is the rare dog who can score 70 in a loss and 110 in a win.

Jeka Wells, $6,600, 79.3 FPPF. The cheapest verified output on the slate, hiding at min price behind Orolbai's shine. At +320 he's a dart, not a pick — but he's a dart with a track record, which beats a dart with a story.

The Empty Premium: Sample-Size Corner

Standing discipline, applied to the top shelf: Orolbai's 111.3 leads the board, on a short UFC sample — his 16-2 is the MMA record, not the fight count behind that average. Priced at $9,600, you're paying full retail for partially verified shine; he's a fine play sized honestly. Lerone Fernando at $9,100 is the Tuchalov-Foro archetype's latest edition: a debutant priced in the premium tier with zero UFC data. Three cards running, the market has cooled on that archetype by lock. The pass costs nothing; the field will carry him for you. Abdul-Malik (-550) and Ribovics (-500) are the market-backed premium chalk — boring, correct, rosterable.

The Assembly

For a 28K-entry PPV field: take the glitch (Johnson — chalk now, but structurally too cheap to skip). Pay the gravity well in most builds and own the Garry outcome in one. Build the middle around the quiet money — Fernandes first, Robertson as the title-fight leverage, Wells as the sanctioned cheap dart. Let the name tax fund you: zero Barboza, minimal Luque, skip the Magny coinflip. Respect the sample-size corner up top. And remember what the banner on the contest page means: on a card where DraftKings itself is announcing the best value, the real edges are the ones without a banner.
