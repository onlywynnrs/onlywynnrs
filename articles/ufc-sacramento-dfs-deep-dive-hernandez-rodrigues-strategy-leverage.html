UFC Sacramento DFS Deep Dive: Hernandez vs Rodrigues Strategy, Picks & Leverage

UFC Sacramento DFS: The Grappler's Main Event, the Tracker's Favorite Fight, and a $9,900 Warning Label

The UFC returns to Sacramento's Golden 1 Center this Saturday — 13 fights, prelims through the afternoon, main card at 8 p.m. ET on Paramount+ — with a slate that hands our playbook three of its favorite patterns at once: a mid-priced main event (both headliners under $8,700), a clean output inversion in a dead coinflip, and the most extreme premium-debutant case we've ever put a number on. Full fight analysis, the ownership map, and three constructed builds below.

The Main Event: Fluffy's Engine vs. the One-Punch Answer

Anthony Hernandez (-189, $8,600) is the kind of favorite this scoring format was built for: a takedown-chaining, scramble-creating pressure grappler with a 96.1 FPPF. His cardio is the weapon — he drowns opponents in volume wrestling until they break late, and his recent run includes submitting the same Roman Dolidze who appears later on this card. Five rounds of that pace is a 110+ point profile when it works.

The counter-case is specific: Gregory Rodrigues ($7,600, +146, 84.9 FPPF) is not a salary-relief dog — he's a knockout artist with a real scoring average whose one path (early concrete) is exactly the path that beats a pressure grappler before the drowning starts. The tape question is simple: does Hernandez survive rounds one and two? Historically yes — his durability through early danger is proven — and every minute past the tenth tilts the fight to the wrestler. The DFS translation: Hernandez is the floor-and-volume side, Rodrigues is the ceiling side, both genuinely playable at mid-tier prices, one per lineup always.

The Tracker's Fight: Padilla at Even Money

The fight our own sharp-money tracker flagged all week, and the salary sheet makes it better. Nasrat Haqparast ($8,200) and Chris Padilla ($8,000) are $200 apart in a fight the books price a dead coinflip (-129/+101). The output column is not a coinflip: Padilla averages 90.1 FPPF to Haqparast's 64.4. The style story backs the number — Padilla is a pace-and-pressure fighter who accumulates in every phase, while Haqparast's cleaner boxing produces lower-volume decisions. Twenty-six points of average output, for less money, at identical win equity, with our tracker's signal on top: this is the Temirov gap, the Fernandes gap, the Bashi gap — the pattern that's hit more reliably than any other read this summer. Padilla is the leverage centerpiece of the card.

The $9,900 Warning Label: Wint at -1099 in a Double-Debut

Anthony Wint is the most expensive fighter on the slate, a -1099 favorite — and a UFC debutant fighting another UFC debutant (Terrance Chatman, $6,300). We've tracked the premium-priced-debutant archetype across five cards now, and the market has cooled on it by lock every time: Tuchalov, Foro, Fernando, all softened while verified-output neighbors held. Wint is the archetype at its maximum: top salary, zero UFC data on either side, priced like a lock. One row down sits Marcio Barbosa ($9,800, -1133) with a real 101.6 FPPF on an 18-2 record — the verified version of the same bet, one hundred dollars cheaper. That comparison is the whole decision.

The Ownership Map: Where the Field Goes

The salary structure repeats Vegas 120: a cheap main event pushes the field's money to the premium tier. Expect the pile on Barbosa (justified — eat it), Douglas (the visible 122 does the advertising), Wint (top price reads as top safety to casuals who don't check the debut), and Hernandez (the name side of the main event). The thin pockets: de Ridder — skid narratives depress casual ownership, so an 80% favorite may go under-owned, the rarest species on any slate; Padilla — the field clicks Haqparast's name recognition from his long UFC run; Nzechukwu and Spivac — heavyweight coinflips the field skips; and the entire McVey-Schultz fight, this card's forgotten bout. The doctrine holds: be different where the crowd is provably wrong (Wint over Barbosa is wrong; Haqparast over Padilla is wrong), eat the chalk where it's right.

Roster Construction: Three Shapes

Build A — The Balanced Spine ($49,300): Barbosa · de Ridder · Hernandez · Padilla · Chaves · Emmers. The verified premium pair plus the under-owned chalk, the main-event floor side, the leverage centerpiece, and two cheap output dogs instead of salary-relief bodies. This is the highest-floor shape that still carries three low-owned pieces.

Build B — The Ceiling Chase ($49,400): Barbosa · Douglas · Rodrigues · Padilla · Nzechukwu · Emmers. Swaps to the main event's knockout side and the slate's two biggest per-point ceilings (Douglas's 122, Rodrigues's concrete), with the heavyweight coinflip discount. Lower floor, first-place shape.

Build C — The Archetype Fade ($49,900): Dyer · de Ridder · Jones · Padilla · Spivac · Chatman. No main event at all — a deliberate fade of the card's two most-owned fighters (Hernandez AND Wint), with the Wint fade expressed at its logical extreme: Chatman at +600 is the direct roster form of the premium-debutant doctrine. If the $9,900 assumption cracks, this lineup is functionally alone.

Across the three: Padilla locked in all (the triple-alignment play), the main event covered both ways plus one fade, Wint zero times, and every dog spot filled with verified output. Zero same-fight conflicts, all caps verified.
