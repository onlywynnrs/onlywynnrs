Stacking Strategies and Ownership Leverage

Unlike NFL or NBA DFS, UFC doesn't have traditional stacking in the correlated sense, but you can still build strategic lineup constructions. Your optimizer should help you balance salary across all six fighters while identifying spots where you can pay up for premium plays versus where you need to save.

For cash games, prioritize fighters with high floors—those likely to accumulate significant striking volume or control time even if they don't finish. For tournaments, shift your optimizer settings to favor fighters with finishing upside, even if they're riskier plays. This weekend's card offers several finish-or-bust fighters who make sense in GPP formats but should be avoided in double-ups and head-to-heads.

Ownership projection is crucial. If your optimizer includes ownership estimates, use them to identify leverage spots where you can differentiate from the field. A fighter projected at 8% ownership who you believe should be 15% represents a massive edge in large-field tournaments.

Optimizing Your Multi-Entry Tournament Strategy

If you're entering multiple lineups into tournaments, your optimizer becomes even more valuable. The goal is creating differentiated lineups that don't overlap too heavily while maintaining exposure to your highest-conviction plays. Set your optimizer to limit player overlap between lineups, ensuring you're not just submitting the same lineup with minor tweaks.

Consider building around different core groups. One lineup might feature the chalk plays everyone will roster, while another fades the popular picks entirely for a contrarian build. Your optimizer can help you construct these varied approaches efficiently without manually building dozens of lineups.

For this weekend's card, identify 2-3 fighters you absolutely want exposure to across most lineups, then let your optimizer fill in the surrounding pieces with different value and upside combinations. This approach balances conviction with diversification.
