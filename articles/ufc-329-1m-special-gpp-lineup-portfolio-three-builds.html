UFC 329 $1M Special: 3 GPP Lineups Built as One Player

Three Entries, One Plan: Building for the UFC 329 $1M Special

DraftKings' $1M UFC 329 Special takes roughly 47,000 entries and pays $200K to first. That field size changes everything about how you build. You are not trying to be right — you're trying to be right while being different from 47,000 other lineups, most of which will be stuffed with McGregor and Steveson. This is how we're building three entries as one player: a shared core of our highest-conviction plays, with deliberate variance spread around them.

First, the signal that anchors this whole portfolio.

The ITD Tell: Reese and Green

Here's a market detail most of the field will never look at. King Green is roughly even money to win his fight — but just +150 to win inside the distance. Zach Reese is about +123 to win, and also around +150 ITD. Run the arithmetic on Green: if he wins about half the time, and the finish price implies roughly a 40% chance of a stoppage win, the market is saying about eight of every ten Green wins come by finish. Reese's numbers say nearly all of his do.

That is the DFS holy grail in one number: underdogs who essentially cannot win a boring decision. When they lose, they score nothing — but you were paying underdog ownership anyway. When they win, they win the way DraftKings pays: +90 first-round bonuses, stoppage points, ceiling. In a 47,000-entry field, a dog whose only winning path is violence is worth more than a favorite whose winning path is fifteen quiet minutes.

Both are in two of our three entries.

The Shared Core

Two fighters appear in all three builds, because conviction should be concentrated where the market agrees with you:

Ryan Gandra ($8,600) — the highest scoring average on the slate (130.6 FPPF) at a near-coinflip price. The honest caveat: that number is a one-fight sample, so treat it as a loud debut signal rather than an established average. Even discounted, the salary-to-output gap is real.

One structural note before the builds: Gandra and Reese are opponents — it's the same fight. They can never share a lineup, so this portfolio takes the Gandra side in two entries and the Reese side in one. Opposite sides of a coinflip across different entries is hedged exposure to a chaotic fight; both sides in one entry is a wasted roster spot.

Damian Pinas ($8,900, 118.9 FPPF) appears twice — the only favorite the market strengthened on all week.

And one fighter appears in zero builds: Conor McGregor. His line collapsed from +185 to +138 on pure name money, and his ownership will run far past his real chances. In a field this size, the McGregor fade is the cheapest leverage available — you get it just by not clicking him.

Entry 1 — The Backbone ($49,700)

Basharat ($9,600) · Pinas ($8,900) · Gandra ($8,600) · Saint Denis ($8,500) · Green ($7,800) · Ellison ($6,300)

The closest thing to an optimal build we'd submit. Basharat against a short-notice replacement is the safest projection on the slate. BSD is the quiet side of the flipped co-main — the market moved to Pimblett, ownership will lag, and the under is still juiced hard: violence either way, at fading ownership. Green is the ITD dog. Ellison is the punt that pays for all of it — and yes, that's a deliberate side effect: if Ellison somehow shocks Steveson, this entry is alone at the top of the world.

Entry 2 — The Dog Train ($48,700)

Holloway ($9,000) · Pinas ($8,900) · Gandra ($8,600) · Green ($7,800) · Krylov ($7,500) · Kamaka ($6,900)

Our one piece of main-event exposure, on the right side of it: Holloway at ~68% with the field piled on the other corner. Around him, the market's fight-week story — Green's ITD profile, Krylov (whose fights essentially never see a scorecard, now a near-coinflip), and Kamaka, whose line closed from -290 to about -200 after our model flagged dog steam midweek. Three live underdogs with finish-only profiles is exactly the shape that wins a 47K-entry tournament and exactly the shape that busts in cash. This is not a cash lineup. It's not supposed to be.

Entry 3 — The Chalk-and-Chaos Split ($49,900)

Steveson ($9,900) · Yanez ($9,400) · Pimblett ($7,700) · Reese ($7,600) · Bautista ($7,900) · Royval ($7,400)

The full-spend barbell, and the Reese entry — he takes the spot here precisely because Gandra doesn't. One entry should own the Steveson outcome — if the Olympian does what -1100 says, two-thirds of our portfolio isn't stranded. Yanez is the premium finisher whose line never wavered. Pimblett is the headline mispricing: salaried as a +114 dog, now favored at some books. Bautista's wrestling pace gives this build a points floor at underdog ownership, and Royval's chaos style scores in every fight he's in.

The Portfolio Logic, Honestly

Across three entries: McGregor zero times. Steveson once. Main event once. Gandra twice, Reese once — never together, because they fight each other. Pinas and Green twice. That's the shape of a real opinion — concentrated where we have conviction, diversified where the card is genuinely chaotic, and deliberately underweight the two fighters 47,000 entries will be built around.

None of this is a promise. Any of these can go 0-for-6 — that's MMA, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What we can promise is that the reasoning is real: live closing odds, the ITD math, and ownership logic for a field this size.
