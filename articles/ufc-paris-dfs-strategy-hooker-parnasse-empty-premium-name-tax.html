UFC Paris DFS: The Most Expensive Fighter on the Board Has Never Fought in the UFC

Before anything else: last week we argued Song Yadong was the leverage play against Umar Nurmagomedov in Shanghai, and then recommended a single-entry lineup with Nurmagomedov in it anyway. Song knocked him out. The read was right and the build was wrong, and that is a worse outcome than simply being wrong, because it means the analysis was there and we did not act on it.

It goes in the tracker as a loss. Now, Paris.

A quarter of this slate has no data

Twenty-eight fighters. Seven of them have never fought in the UFC.

That is not unusual in itself. What is unusual is where they sit. The most expensive fighter on the entire board is Salahdine Parnasse at $9,500, and DraftKings lists his fantasy points per fight as a dash, because there is nothing to list. He has never been in the Octagon. The UFC has handed a debutant a five-round main event, which almost never happens, and DraftKings has priced him like an established star.

It gets stranger further down. Two entire fights are debut versus debut. Fabia Sintes and Michael Aljarouj have no UFC data between them. Neither do Delphine Benouaich and Sofia Montenegro. Four fighters, two fights, zero projection history on either side of either bout.

Any projection you see for those eight fighters, including ours, is a model output derived from the betting line. It is not a measurement. Treat it accordingly.

Two of the three priciest favorites score twenty points

This is the sharpest edge on the card and it is sitting in plain sight.

Kurtis Campbell is priced at $9,300 as a -410 favorite. His fantasy points per fight is 20.3. Losene Keita is $9,200 as a -399 favorite. His is 20.8.

Those are the second and third most expensive fighters on the slate, and they produce roughly a fifth of what the top scorers produce. Run it per thousand dollars of salary and Campbell returns 2.18 points, Keita 2.26. The slate leader returns 13.56.

Now look across the cage. Campbell's opponent Trevor Peek costs $6,900 and scores 62.4. Keita's opponent Muhammad Naimov costs $7,000 and scores 68.7. Both underdogs produce roughly three times the fantasy output of the favorite they are facing, at about a quarter less salary.

This is the Empty Premium in its purest form. DraftKings is charging for win probability, and win probability is not what scores points. Grinding out a decision as a heavy favorite gets you the win and gets your lineup very little. A slate where two of the three most expensive fighters have sub-25 fantasy averages is a slate where the pricing model and the scoring model have come apart.

You do not need to fade Campbell and Keita because they will lose. They will probably both win. You fade them because winning is not worth $9,300 when it comes with twenty points attached.

The Name Tax has a face this week

Michael Page is $8,700 with a 41.2 fantasy average. Nursulton Ruziboev, the man across from him, is $7,500 with 83.2.

Ruziboev scores twice as much for $1,200 less, and he is a live underdog at +130 rather than a longshot. Page is one of the most recognizable fighters in Europe and has been for a decade, and that recognition is the entire justification for the price gap. That is the Name Tax, and it is rarely this legible.

Three coin flips and one easy answer

Three fights on this card are priced at essentially even money. Sintes and Aljarouj sit at -101 and -126. Cornolle and Sygula at -126 and -101. Dias and Duclos at -110 and -115.

Our rule for a genuine coin flip is that output per dollar breaks the tie, because if the market cannot separate two fighters on who wins, the only remaining question is who scores if they do.

Two of these three resolve cleanly under that rule and one does not. Cornolle at $8,300 scores 54.9; Sygula at $7,900 scores 63.7, so Sygula wins on both price and output. And Luis Felipe Dias at $8,000 carries a 108.5 fantasy average against Matthieu Duclos, who is a debutant with no average at all, at a nearly identical $8,200. That is the best value on the entire slate, and it is available in a fight the market calls a toss-up. Sintes and Aljarouj cannot be resolved this way at all, because neither has any output to compare, which is exactly why we are not playing that fight.

The optimizer is about to hand everyone the same lineup

We enumerated every legal six-fighter lineup on this slate. There are 75,829 of them.

Five names appear in essentially every top-ceiling build: Mario Pinto, Daniil Donchenko, Luis Felipe Dias, Axel Sola, and Nursulton Ruziboev. That is not a coincidence and it is not an edge. It means the projections point so strongly at those five that any optimizer, fed any reasonable numbers, arrives at the same core.

In a fourteen-thousand-entry tournament, that is a duplication problem. If you build the mathematically optimal lineup here, you will not be the only one holding it, and a shared first-place ticket is worth a fraction of a sole one.

The differentiation therefore has to come from the sixth slot, not from breaking up the core. Bad differentiation is swapping out one of the five for someone who does not belong. Good differentiation is choosing a final piece the field will not.

Where we are going with the last spot

Dan Hooker is $6,700, the second-cheapest fighter on the board, with a 68.6 fantasy average, fighting five rounds.

Five rounds matters more than people account for. Two extra rounds is two extra rounds of significant strikes, takedown attempts, and control time in a scoring system that rewards all three. The cheapest meaningful path to a high ceiling on this card runs through the main event underdog, not around him.

The honest counterweight: we just got burned taking the favorite over the underdog in Shanghai, and there is a well-documented tendency for the field, and for analysts, to arrive dog-drunk the week after a big upset. We have written about that overcorrection before. It would be easy to talk ourselves onto Hooker as penance.

So separate the two. Hooker's case does not rest on what happened last Saturday. It rests on $6,700 for a five-round fighter averaging 68.6, opposite a $9,500 opponent whose UFC fantasy output is a blank line. That argument was true before Shanghai and would be true if Umar had won.

What we are not doing is loading up on underdogs across the board because one hit last week. The Empty Premium fades are about pricing, not about a mood.

The short version

Fade Campbell and Keita at premium prices for twenty-point averages. Pay the least possible for the Page fight and take Ruziboev instead. Take Dias in the coin flip because he is the only one of the four with a measured ceiling. Do not play the two debut-versus-debut bouts at all. And accept that the core of this slate is obvious, then spend your differentiation on the last roster spot rather than pretending the obvious core is wrong.
