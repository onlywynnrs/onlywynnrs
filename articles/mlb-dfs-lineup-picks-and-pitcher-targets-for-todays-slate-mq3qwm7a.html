Cash Game vs Tournament Construction

Your lineup construction approach should differ dramatically between cash games and GPPs. Cash game lineups prioritize floor and consistency—you want hitters with high contact rates, favorable platoon splits, and spots in proven offensive lineups.

For today's cash games, focus on the Yankees-Red Sox game for your primary stack. Both offenses have demonstrated consistent run production, and the game environment suggests scoring opportunities. Pair this stack with a safe SP1 who has a strikeout floor above seven and limited walk concerns.

Tournament lineups require differentiation. Consider contrarian stacks from the Tigers or Marlins that most players will fade. Ownership projections matter significantly in large-field GPPs—a four-man stack at 8% ownership that scores 45+ points can vault you into the money.

Correlation plays also provide tournament upside. If you're playing an underdog team stack, pair it with the opposing pitcher. When the underdog scores runs but still loses, you capture points from both sides. The Mariners pitcher paired with Tigers bats exemplifies this correlation strategy.

Weather and Late-Breaking News

Before finalizing your lineups, monitor weather conditions and lineup confirmations. Wind blowing out at 10+ mph in the Yankees-Red Sox game would significantly boost run expectations and make hitter stacks more appealing. Conversely, strong winds blowing in could suppress scoring and make pitchers safer plays.

Check lineup announcements 60-90 minutes before lock. Players moving up in the batting order gain significant value through additional plate appearance opportunities. A hitter jumping from seventh to second in the order for minimum price represents the best value play on the slate.

Starting pitcher confirmations matter too. If a projected starter gets scratched, the replacement is often significantly worse, making the opposing offense a smash-stack opportunity. Set lineup alerts to catch these late-breaking changes that create leverage.
