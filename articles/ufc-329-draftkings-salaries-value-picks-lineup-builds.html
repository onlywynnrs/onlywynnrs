UFC 329 DraftKings Salaries: Value Picks, Leverage & Lineup Builds

The $50K Problem, Final Edition: Salaries Locked, Odds Moved

DraftKings salaries were set early in fight week. The odds kept moving. That gap between a frozen price sheet and a live market is where DFS value comes from — and after clean weigh-ins (all 28 made weight), the final map looks meaningfully different from the opening one. Average build: $8,333 per roster spot. Here's the tier-by-tier last look.

The Headline Mispricing: Pimblett at $7,700

When salaries were set, Paddy Pimblett was a +114 underdog and priced like one. As of Friday, the market has flipped the co-main — Pimblett is favored at some books, a true pick'em everywhere else. That means DK is selling a fighter the market now favors, with a 93-point career FPPF, at underdog-tier salary in a fight where the under is heavily juiced. Salary sheets can't reprice mid-week; this is the single biggest gap between price and market on the slate. The flip side: Benoit Saint Denis ($8,500, 101.0 FPPF) now costs $800 more for the side the market demoted — but ownership lags lines, and if the field still treats BSD as the streaking favorite, he quietly becomes the leverage half of the same violent fight.

The Premium Tier ($8,900+): One Number Softened

Gable Steveson ($9,900) is still the most expensive fighter on the board, but his line walked back from the -2800 neighborhood to nearer -1100 — the market cooling slightly on debut euphoria, though 92% is still 92%. He weighed a fit 241 to Ellison's 236. Cash: play him. GPP: the fade remains the slate's biggest single leverage lever, now with marginally better justification.

Farid Basharat ($9,600) vs. a short-notice replacement is unchanged — the safest projection on the card (86.4 FPPF, 15-0), fully priced for it.

Adrian Yanez ($9,400) held around -350 all week and stays our favorite premium tournament buy: genuine finisher, opponent's chin repeatedly cracked, and the market never wavered.

Max Holloway ($9,000) made 170 comfortably in his first welterweight cut — the one physical question, answered. Around a 68% favorite in a five-round fight with an 86.2 FPPF, he's the premium spot we like most, and the all-week McGregor money only improves his relative ownership.

Alessandro Costa ($9,100) softened from -250 to about -200 — the market pricing in his two-week camp. His 2026 form (two TKOs, the last in under three minutes) still says anchor; the closing move says the field's doubts are real. GPP piece, not cash lock.

The Value Belt ($8,300–$8,900): Still Where the Slate Is Won

Damian Pinas ($8,900, 118.9 FPPF) got stronger — the only favorite the market added to all week. Second-highest scoring average on the slate, now with the line confirming. He's the closest thing to a lock-button in the value belt.

Ryan Gandra ($8,600, 130.6 FPPF) — the highest FPPF on the card at a near-coinflip price. Small sample, but the mispricing argument is unchanged. In nearly every build.

Terrance McKinney ($8,400) is now a dead-even fight — pick'em from -148. Same boom-or-bust chaos, but the market just told you his win probability is 50%, not 60%. The GPP case survives (his finish rate is the point); the cash case weakened.

Cory Sandhagen ($8,300, 89.2 FPPF) — unchanged, and still the value-belt trap: elite fighter, decision-heavy scoring profile. Fade in GPPs.

The Leverage Zone ($7,200–$8,000): The Market Agreed With the Dogs

Fight week validated this tier. Nikita Krylov ($7,500) closed near +113 — a genuine coinflip priced $800 under slate average, whose every win is a finish. The best leverage-per-dollar on the board. King Green ($7,800) is now even money against McKinney with the field mostly on the other side. Mario Bautista ($7,900) and Brandon Royval ($7,400) still carry FPPFs that outrun their prices.

Conor McGregor ($7,200) is the anti-leverage warning, doubled: his line collapsed from +185 to about +138 on relentless name money, which means his ownership will run even further past his real 42% chance. Higher career FPPF than Holloway, cheaper, more famous — the field will see all of it. Riding McGregor is riding with the crowd at a worse price than the market gives him.

The Punt Bin: One Got Interesting

Cody Durden ($7,100) closed from +215 to about +200 with our model flagging dog steam on him midweek — the market genuinely moved his way. A durable veteran against a two-week-notice camp, at near-minimum ownership: he's graduated from "word before the punts" to the most defensible dart under $7,200. Kai Kamaka III ($6,900) got the same treatment — Riley's line fell from -290 to about -200, and Kamaka is live. Garbrandt ($6,800) remains a one-punch lottery ticket; Garza ($6,600) and Ellison ($6,300) remain salary-relief buttons.

Three Final Builds

Salaries never moved, so the frames hold — with sharper reasoning behind each:

The Cash Frame: Basharat ($9,600) + Holloway ($9,000) + Pinas ($8,900) + BSD ($8,500) + Krylov ($7,500) + Ellison ($6,300) = $49,800. Pinas and Krylov both got stronger all week.

The Balanced GPP: Yanez ($9,400) + Pinas ($8,900) + Gandra ($8,600) + McKinney ($8,400) + Pimblett ($7,700) + Garbrandt ($6,800) = $49,800. Now carries the slate's headline mispricing (Pimblett) at its center.

The Leverage Swing: Holloway ($9,000) + Gandra ($8,600) + BSD ($8,500) + Sandhagen ($8,300) + Krylov ($7,500) + Kamaka ($6,900) = $48,800. Fades both crowd magnets (Steveson, McGregor) and holds two fighters the market bet all week (Krylov, Kamaka).

These aren't the six who will win — they're frames for where the frozen salary sheet is now most out of line with a market that kept moving.
