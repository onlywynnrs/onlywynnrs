UFC 329 DraftKings Salaries: Value Picks, Leverage & Lineup Builds

The $50K Problem: Building UFC 329 Lineups With Real Salaries

Our UFC 329 strategy piece covered the card's shape — the McGregor ownership trap, the Steveson chalk question, where the finishes live. Now the DraftKings salaries are out, and salaries change everything. A great play at the wrong price is a bad play. Here's the value map for Saturday's 28-fighter, $50,000-cap slate, and the honest math behind each tier.

One number to keep in mind throughout: the average build is $8,333 per roster spot. Every dollar you spend above that on one fighter has to be earned back below it somewhere else.

The Pricing Story DraftKings Is Telling

The most interesting thing about this salary sheet is what's priced at the top. Gable Steveson ($9,900) and Farid Basharat ($9,600) — two prelim hammers — are the most expensive fighters on the slate, priced above Max Holloway ($9,000), who headlines the card. DraftKings is charging you for certainty, not star power. The site knows the field wants safe wins, and it's taxing them accordingly.

Meanwhile Conor McGregor sits at just $7,200 — below slate average — despite being the biggest name in the sport. That's not generosity. It's a fair price on a +185 underdog coming off a five-year layoff. The question is whether the field treats it as a discount anyway.

The Premium Tier ($8,900+): Pay for Finishes, Not Safety

Max Holloway ($9,000) is the premium spot we like most. A 69% favorite in a five-round fight with an 86.2 career FPPF — the five-round format is the hidden value, because volume compounds over 25 minutes and Holloway is the highest-volume striker of his era. If it goes long and he wins, he scores like a finish anyway.

Gable Steveson ($9,900) is the priciest fighter on the board with zero UFC scoring history. The market says fast finish (-2800, under 1.5 rounds heavily juiced). If that happens, he pays it off. But $9,900 for a debut is the field paying maximum price for an assumption — and it eats 20% of your cap. Cash: fine. Tournaments: this is the single best place on the slate to be different.

Farid Basharat ($9,600) against a short-notice replacement is the safest projection on the card (86.4 FPPF, 15-0). The honest question is whether "safest" justifies second-most-expensive. In cash, yes. In GPPs, you're paying a premium the whole field is also paying.

Adrian Yanez ($9,400) is a cleaner tournament buy at the top: a genuine finisher against a chin that's been cracked repeatedly, priced below the two safety plays.

The Value Belt ($8,300–$8,900): Where This Slate Is Won

This is the richest stretch of the salary sheet, and it's not close.

Damian Pinas ($8,900, 118.9 FPPF) — the second-highest scoring average on the slate, priced $1,000 below Steveson. A -218 favorite with a finishing profile. This is the kind of number that builds itself into lineups.

Ryan Gandra ($8,600, 130.6 FPPF) — the highest FPPF on the entire card, tucked in the prelims at a near-coinflip price (-135). Small sample (9-1), but a 130-point average at $8,600 is the definition of salary-sheet mispricing. He's in nearly every build we'd make.

Benoit Saint Denis ($8,500, 101.0 FPPF) — a triple-digit average in the co-main, where the under is juiced hard. The market expects violence; BSD has finished four straight. At mid-tier salary, he's the bridge that lets you fit a premium anchor.

Terrance McKinney ($8,400) — boom-or-bust at a fair price. His fights end in minutes, one way or the other. GPP-only, but the +25 quick-win bonus profile is exactly what wins tournaments.

Cory Sandhagen ($8,300, 89.2 FPPF) — the trap in the value belt. Great average, elite fighter, but this matchup profiles as fifteen technical minutes, and a decision win scores +30. Fine in cash, fade-able in GPPs.

The Leverage Zone ($7,200–$8,000): The Dogs That Matter

Conor McGregor ($7,200, 88.2 FPPF) — here's the uncomfortable math: his career scoring average is higher than Holloway's, he costs $1,800 less, and he's the most famous athlete on the card. The field will see all of that too. As we said in the strategy piece, McGregor will carry ownership far above his 35% win probability — which makes him negative leverage despite the friendly price. If you play him, know you're riding with the crowd, not against it.

Paddy Pimblett ($7,700, 93.0 FPPF) — the real leverage in the co-main. Near-coinflip odds (+114), a 93-point average, in a fight the market expects to end early. If the BSD side is over-owned because of the streak narrative, Pimblett at this price is the sharp side of the same violence.

Nikita Krylov ($7,500) — his fights essentially never see a scorecard. A live dog (+120) whose every win is a finish, at $800 below average cost. Classic GPP leverage.

Mario Bautista ($7,900, 79.4 FPPF) and Brandon Royval ($7,400, 74.1 FPPF) — both carry averages that outrun their prices, both are live. Bautista's wrestling pace gives him a points floor even in a decision; Royval's chaos style scores in every fight he's in.

King Green ($7,800) — the other half of the McKinney violence. Cheaper, live, and the field will mostly be on the McKinney side.

The Punt Bin (below $7,000): Handle Honestly

Kai Kamaka III ($6,900) and Cody Garbrandt ($6,800) are the only punts with realistic paths — Kamaka is durable against a hyped prospect, and Garbrandt's counter-power is the one thing that never left him. John Garza ($6,600), the short-notice debutant, and Elisha Ellison ($6,300), a +1300 underdog with a 1.6 FPPF, are salary-relief buttons, not plays. Every punt you roster needs the savings to buy a genuine ceiling elsewhere — otherwise you've just made your lineup worse at both ends.

Three Honest Builds

The Cash Frame: Basharat ($9,600) + Holloway ($9,000) + Pinas ($8,900) + BSD ($8,500) + Krylov ($7,500) + Ellison ($6,300) = $49,800. Safety up top, one live dog, one punt to pay for it.

The Balanced GPP: Yanez ($9,400) + Pinas ($8,900) + Gandra ($8,600) + McKinney ($8,400) + Pimblett ($7,700) + Garbrandt ($6,800) = $49,800. No Steveson, no main event — pure finish-hunting through the value belt.

The Leverage Swing: Steveson-fade, McGregor-fade double: Holloway ($9,000) + Gandra ($8,600) + BSD ($8,500) + Sandhagen ($8,300) + Krylov ($7,500) + Kamaka ($6,900) = $48,800, leaving cap room to pivot any spot up. Different from the field in the two places the field is most concentrated.

These aren't the six fighters who will win — nobody knows that. They're frames for thinking about where the salary sheet is generous and where it's taxing you.
